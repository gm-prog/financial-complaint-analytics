# Project Description — Financial Customer Complaint Analytics

This project focused on understanding financial customer complaints and turning operational records into actionable service insights. The objective was to examine complaint volumes, open and closed cases, issue categories, financial products, company-level patterns, submission channels, geographic distribution, and response timeliness. The work combined spreadsheet-based data review, SQL reporting/aggregation, KPI calculation and validation, and an interactive Power BI dashboard.

## Dataset and preparation

The supplied project file contained 5,000 rows and 14 fields, including complaint ID, date received, financial product, issue category and detail, company name, state, submission channel, response due date, company response date, response status, response outcome, timely-response flag, and a short complaint summary. The date coverage ran from 1 October 2025 through 15 October 2026.

Data quality was treated as part of the analysis rather than as a preliminary formality. The 25 duplicate complaint-ID rows were exact duplicates; duplicate-ID groups were checked for conflicting field values before retaining one copy. This produced 4,975 unique complaint records. Text labels were trimmed and normalised so variations in capitalisation and spacing could be grouped consistently. Date fields were parsed and the response-due interval was checked. All 4,975 unique records had a due date 15 calendar days after receipt, and all 4,665 closed records had a timely/late flag consistent with the date comparison. The 49 missing state values were retained as unknown instead of being assigned an assumed location. Missing response dates and timely flags on open records were treated as expected status-related blanks.

## Analysis and tools

Excel was used for initial inspection and operational-data review. SQL reporting aggregates/views were used to structure analysis by company, product, issue category, state, channel, and month. The reporting objects used in the project were `VW_COMPANY_BACKLOG_PERFORMANCE`, `VW_PRODUCT_BACKLOG_PERFORMANCE`, `VW_ISSUE_BACKLOG_PERFORMANCE`, `VW_STATE_RESPONSE_PERFORMANCE`, `VW_CHANNEL_RESPONSE_PERFORMANCE`, and `VW_MONTHLY_COMPLAINT_TRENDS`. Power BI was used to build a two-page report: an Executive Overview page containing KPI cards and monthly trend analysis, and an Operational Analysis page containing company, product, issue, state, and response-channel visuals. Slicers were added for company, financial product, state, and month.

The analysis calculated total, open, and closed complaint counts; open complaint rate; on-time and late response counts and rates; average response time; and overdue-age measures for unresolved cases. It also compared both counts and denominators. This distinction matters: the product with the largest number of open cases is not necessarily the product with the highest open rate, and the channel with the largest late count should be compared using its own closed-case total.

## Main results

After exact duplicate removal, the dataset contained 4,975 unique complaints: 310 open (6.23%) and 4,665 closed. Of closed cases, 4,202 were on time (90.08%) and 463 were late (9.92%). The average time from receipt to the recorded company response for closed cases was 8.76 calendar days, with a median of eight days.

Open-case ageing was the most significant operational concern. Using 20 November 2026 as the reporting date, all 310 open cases had passed the supplied response due date. A total of 240 cases (77.4% of the open backlog) were at least 90 days overdue. Average overdue age was 205.3 days, the median was 201.5 days, and the oldest case was 394 days overdue. This indicates that the unresolved inventory needs case-level prioritisation, named ownership, and escalation—not just a headline open-case count.

Unexpected Fees was the largest open issue category with 35 cases, followed by Billing Dispute with 25 and Account Access with 23. Together these three categories represented 83 cases, or 26.8% of the open backlog. Interest Charges contributed another 18. These categories identify areas for root-cause review, but their labels alone do not prove that fees were incorrectly charged or that a company process failed.

Credit Card had the largest absolute open count (73). Buy Now, Pay Later had the highest product-level open rate: 33 open cases from 350 complaints, or 9.43%. Phone had 130 late responses among 1,168 closed Phone cases, an 11.13% late rate—the highest of the four submission channels. Mail's late rate was 9.93%, Web's 9.69%, and Referral's 8.96%. Phone should therefore be investigated first, while case mix, issue complexity, and staffing patterns should be examined before attributing the gap to a single cause.

Monthly intake ranged from 352 to 433 complaints for the complete months shown. May 2026 had the highest full-month intake at 433. October 2026 contained only 212 records through 15 October and is a partial month; it should not be compared directly with full months.

## Recommendations

1. **Reduce fee and billing-related complaints.** Review a sample of open and recently closed Unexpected Fees, Billing Dispute, and Interest Charges cases by product, company, and issue detail. Map complaint evidence to fee triggers, customer disclosures, billing events, and system configuration. Add pre-bill validation and exception alerts where a verified root cause justifies them. A proposed target is a 20% reduction in the combined monthly count of these three categories within six months, measured against a verified rolling baseline.
2. **Run an aged-backlog recovery programme.** Create one case-level queue for the 310 open records with a named owner, overdue age, due date, latest customer contact, next action, blocker, and escalation date. Prioritise 90+ day cases, provide clear customer updates, and review progress daily/weekly. A proposed 90-day target is to reduce the 90+ day backlog from 240 to 120 or fewer while preserving evidence of genuine resolution and tracking reopened cases.
3. **Improve Phone-channel timeliness and review BNPL cases.** Examine Phone queue staffing by time of day, routing, transfers, repeat contacts, case complexity, and after-call work. Introduce alerts before due dates and proactive callbacks for cases approaching a breach. In parallel, sample BNPL open cases to understand whether payment posting, fees, access, dispute documentation, or partner hand-offs contribute to the higher open rate. A proposed target is to improve Phone on-time performance from 88.87% to at least 95% within 90 days, subject to operational approval.

## Outcome and limitations

The project produced a cleaned analytical population, a KPI validation summary, category and channel findings, a two-page Power BI design, a final written report, and three measurable improvement proposals. The report uses the supplied dataset only; it does not establish that the company labels represent actual financial institutions or that the records are a complete or representative sample. There is no customer identifier for repeat-customer analysis, no full multi-stage correspondence history, and no evidence in the CSV to adjudicate complaint narratives. Ageing is a snapshot as of 20 November 2026 and should be recalculated on refresh. The original executed SQL statements and final PBIX were not independently audited as source artefacts when the report was prepared; the numerical KPIs were independently recalculated from the supplied CSV.
